### [Redshifted Cool Gas in the Disturbed HI Disk of the Starburst Dwarf NGC 4214](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13039v1)

Shulan Yan, Taotao Fang, Xiaochuan Jiang, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Combining archival HST/COS, Keck/HIRES, VLA–THINGS, and FAST HI data toward the starburst dwarf **NGC 4214**, the authors identify a coherent **redshifted low-ionization absorption system** (S II, Mg II, Ca II, offset 20–30 km/s) in front of the central UV/optical continuum, plus a matching redshifted HI emission component. Because the foreground absorbing gas shows a positive velocity offset, the geometry is most naturally read as **inflow or recycled fallback** rather than outflow, with a fiducial inflow rate of **~1.7 M☉/yr (~13× the current SFR)**. FAST mapping further reveals a large-scale warped, kinematically disturbed HI disk, though the authors stress non-circular/warp motions cannot be excluded as an alternative explanation.

![](figures/2610.13039/HI_UV_spec_CaII_fullrange_sameylim.pdf)

*VLA HI spectrum decomposed into local, redshifted, and intermediate velocity components (top), with the normalized COS/HIRES absorption-line fits (S II, Mg II, Ca II) and their fitted centroids below — the direct evidential basis for the claimed inflow signature.*

### [Exploring Case B I: The Footprints of Hidden Radiation Fields in H I Balmer Lines](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12715v1)

Maryam Dehghanian, Fransisco Guzman, Peter van Hoof, et al.

**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using the **Cloudy** photoionization code with an extended hydrogen model atom, the authors test whether an additional soft, non-ionizing external radiation field (mimicking an older/cooler stellar population co-located with an H II region) can explain observed departures from **Case B** recombination seen in some high-redshift JWST systems. Blackbody continua with **log(T_eff/K) ~ 3.2–4.2** most strongly perturb the Balmer cascade via **photoexcitation from the metastable 2s level**, enhancing Hα/Hβ while suppressing Hγ/Hβ along a trajectory **distinct from the dust-reddening sequence** — giving a clean diagnostic to separate reddening from a hidden cool radiation field. The mechanism cannot explain the most extreme low-Hα/Hβ systems, implying additional physics is still needed for some objects.

![](figures/2610.12715/RatiosBB.pdf)

*Hα/Hβ and Hγ/Hβ as functions of the perturbing blackbody's effective temperature at three densities, with Case B reference lines, showing exactly where and how strongly continuum pumping departs from the canonical ratio.*

### [The Fast & Fourier: Using neural operators to speed up radiative models of protoplanetary disks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12632v1)

Lukas Welzel, Melissa K. McClure
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.SR

The authors train a **Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)** as a surrogate for RADMC-3D radiative-transfer models of ice-bearing, edge-on protoplanetary disks, using a 41,658-model Sobol-sampled training grid built at ~120 CPU-years of cost. The surrogate reproduces simulated spectra with a **median per-spectrum relative error of 4.84%**, shows no detectable bias at nominal **TARP coverage** in a full Bayesian validation, and achieves a **10⁹–10¹⁰× speed-up**, enabling nested-sampling inference in place of hand-tuned grid fits. Applied to the real disk ESO-Hα 569, the surrogate-based posterior yields a massive, nearly edge-on disk consistent with an independent HST+SED analysis — computed in under 2 hours, opening the door to population-level Bayesian studies of disk ice reservoirs.

![](figures/2610.12632/methodology_overview_side_caption.pdf)

*Workflow schematic linking the RADMC-3D training grid, the FNO surrogate, and its use inside the nested-sampling inference loop — the clearest single picture of how the emulator replaces the simulator end to end.*

### [Unifying spherical convolutions through lifting](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13892v1)

J. D. McEwen
**Primary category:** math-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

McEwen develops a unified theory of spherical convolution by treating the sphere as the homogeneous space S²≅SO(3)/SO(2): a spin-s field is **lifted** to the rotation group, convolved there with an arbitrary kernel, and **projected** back to the sphere at a possibly different spin, acting in harmonic space as multiplication by a single Wigner coefficient. This **generalized lifted spherical convolution** is proven to recover every previously proposed rotation-equivariant spherical convolution (axisymmetric, directional, group, spin) as a special case, and the paper shows that directional kernels, spherical output, and equivariance cannot all be achieved simultaneously for fixed input/output spin. The operators are implemented in the open-source, differentiable **`s2conv`** JAX code, with equivariance confirmed to machine precision (~10⁻¹⁵) and demonstrated on CMB polarization and weak-lensing shear.

![](figures/2610.13892/lifted_convolution.pdf)

*The lift–convolve–project pipeline for a spin s=2→s'=1 field: lifted to a function on the rotation group, convolved there, and projected back to a different-spin field on the sphere — shown both geometrically and in harmonic space, where only one Wigner coefficient of the kernel survives.*

### [Bridging Human and Model Attention: Explainability Analysis of CNN, Mamba, and Transformer Architectures via Eye-Tracking](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12703v1)

Marco Stefani, Edoardo Parpaiola, Matteo Bino, et al.
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.HC, cs.LG

The authors benchmark how well post-hoc saliency maps from **CNNs, Vision Transformers, and Vision Mamba** align with human visual attention, using eye-tracking gaze as ground truth across fine-grained classification, medical imaging, and facial-emotion domains. They formalize the comparison with a simplex-projection normalization and three complementary similarity functionals (**Jensen–Shannon, Chi-square, Pearson**), showing these capture distinct, non-redundant properties of an attribution map. The headline finding is an architecture-dependent dissociation: Vision Mamba attains near-maximal gaze overlap on medical images (**JSS = 0.82**) while simultaneously showing **near-zero Pearson correlation (−0.01)** — it looks in the right place but its intensity profile is uncorrelated with human attention, a distinction a single metric would conceal. Backbone freezing improves alignment on medical images at the cost of accuracy, showing the two are not monotonically related.

### [Local Diagnostics of Learned Event Representations for Anomaly Detection at the LHC](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13437v1)

Donatella Genovese, Philip Ploner, Simone Scardapane, et al.
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: hep-ex, physics.data-an

Working on a 750-million-event HL-LHC simulation dataset, the authors train Transformer event embeddings with contrastive objectives and run anomaly detection via an autoencoder's reconstruction error in the learned latent space. Supervised contrastive training (never using signal labels) gives the most informative embeddings, lifting a held-out signal's detection efficiency at 10% background acceptance from a raw-feature baseline of 77.3% to **97.2%**. The core contribution is a **local diagnostic pipeline**: a Gaussian Mixture Model partitions the background latent space into label-free regions, ranks observables by **Wasserstein-1 distance** to the local background, and correlates the anomaly score with those observables — revealing that "what flags an anomaly" and "what the detector score is sensitive to" can be logically distinct diagnostics, a cautionary and directly transferable lesson for anomaly search in image embeddings.

![](figures/2610.13437/end_to_end_pipeline.pdf)

*End-to-end pipeline: encoder → embedding → GMM partitioning + autoencoder anomaly flagging → local diagnostic ranking — a clean one-glance summary of the full method.*

### [SLICE: Sequential Likelihood-Free Inference via Conformal E-Values](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13855v1)

Stefano Cortinovis, Laura Battaglia, François Caron
**Primary category:** stat.ME | also: stat.ML

**SLICE** builds exact, **anytime-valid confidence sequences** for simulation-based (likelihood-free) inference by conformalizing a predictable score (e.g., a neural ratio estimate) against fresh simulations at every incoming observation, multiplying the resulting e-values into a test martingale that stays valid under continuous monitoring and data-dependent stopping — unlike fixed-sample methods. Applied to **5,790 OGLE-IV microlensing events**, the neural-score confidence sequence for the **stellar initial mass function slope θ₁ = −1.13** shrinks steadily and contains the published value in 97% of random orderings, with efficiency close to an exact KDE-based likelihood-ratio score, while the brown-dwarf slope remains only weakly constrained given the low detection rate of short-timescale events.

![](figures/2610.13855/ogle_microlensing.pdf)

*Empirical miscoverage of the method's confidence sequences staying below the nominal rate, alongside the real OGLE-IV confidence sequences for both IMF slopes shrinking toward the published fit as more microlensing events accumulate.*

### [A Relationship Between Fast Radio Burst Dispersion Measure and Host Galaxy Inclination](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13062v1)

Lachlan Marnoch, Stuart D. Ryder, Sunil Simha, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Fitting VLT/FORS2 imaging of 34 CRAFT FRB host galaxies with GALFIT Sérsic models and comparing axis ratios to a ~470,000-galaxy reference catalogue, the authors find **no detection bias against edge-on FRB hosts**, contradicting a prior claim, but instead a **statistically significant correlation between host inclination and the host dispersion measure**, robust across sample cuts. This supports a predominantly **disk-based origin for FRBs** over an extended halo/globular-cluster channel, and using the inclination–DM relation as an empirical correction reduces the RMS scatter about the theoretical Macquart relation from 181 to **107 pc cm⁻³ — a 40% reduction**.

### [When dust growth takes over: the origin of dust in post-reionization galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12591v1)

Omima Osman, Gabriella De Lucia, Andreas L. Faisst, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Running the semi-analytic model **GAEA/DUSTY-GAEA** on Millennium-simulation merger trees, the authors model dust enrichment in massive star-forming galaxies at **z ~ 4.5–5.7** and show the model only reproduces the observed dust mass–stellar mass relation and dust ratios once **efficient dust growth in the ISM** is included — stellar production alone, even at maximal efficiency, cannot supply the observed reservoirs. They identify a **transition redshift at z ~ 8–9** below which ISM growth overtakes stellar dust production, and find these galaxies preferentially sit in environments **14–20× the cosmic mean density**.

### [The Role of Observational and Numerical Biases in Measuring the Star Formation Efficiency per Free-fall Time](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12729v1)

Alexander Escamilla, Michael Y. Grudić, Anna L. Rosen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.SR

Generating synthetic far-infrared dust maps and YSO catalogs from the **STARFORGE** radiation-MHD simulation of a giant molecular cloud, the authors apply real observational methodology to measure the star-formation efficiency per free-fall time, **ε_ff**, finding it naturally reproduces the observed low values (**~1–3%**) from feedback regulation alone, with no need for extreme initial conditions. ε_ff varies strongly over a cloud's ~10 Myr lifetime, showing much of the observed cloud-to-cloud scatter reflects **evolutionary sampling** rather than intrinsic differences, with spatial resolution — not YSO mass/lifetime assumptions — the dominant observational systematic.

### [An empirical calibration of the spectral resolution of JWST NIRSpec PRISM](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13492v1)

Hang Zhou, Xin Wang, Yuxuan Pang, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Using a large sample of [O III] doublet detections from the DAWN JWST Archive's NIRSpec PRISM spectra spanning z ~ 0.5–10, the authors empirically calibrate the PRISM's wavelength- and source-morphology-dependent spectral resolution. The true in-flight resolution **exceeds the pre-launch curve by a factor of 1.03–1.63** depending on source extent, bracketed between point-source and extended-source limits and validated against independent calibration data. The resulting analytic **R(λ, FWHM)** formula is offered as a practical resolution estimator for any statistical PRISM galaxy study — including of dwarf galaxies.

### [ASKAP Monitoring of the Rubin Deep Drilling Fields](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13011v1)

Dougal Dobie, James Freeburn, Iris de Ruiter, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors describe a new ASKAP radio survey monitoring the five **Rubin Observatory Deep Drilling Fields** on a ~10-day cadence with a fully automated, near-real-time alert pipeline — the first imaging-based radio transient survey to achieve public data release on this timescale. ## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Nailing down the Hubble constant value through time delay cosmography: the extended case of SN Refsdal](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12595v1)

S. Schuldt, C. Grillo, S. H. Suyu, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Extending the strong-lens mass model of the cluster hosting SN Refsdal by incorporating the **extended source surface brightness** of 77,000 HST pixels together with measured image time delays, the authors obtain **H₀ = 65.0⁺²·⁰₋₁.₈ km/s/Mpc**, a **3.0% relative uncertainty** — roughly twice as tight as the point-image-only model and the most precise single-strong-lens H₀ measurement to date, surpassing the previous best (3.9%). The result agrees with prior SN Refsdal analyses, other lensed-supernova measurements, and Planck, with the authors noting time-delay measurement precision — not the mass model — is now the dominant remaining uncertainty.

### [The Silk Nebula: A Newly Discovered Giant [OIII] Emission Filament](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13329v1)

Yi-Fei Wu, Songyu Jia, Chao-Jian Wu, et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using [OIII] narrow-band imaging plus 2,684 LAMOST fiber spectra, the authors characterize a newly discovered **[OIII] filamentary structure spanning roughly 1°×10°** on the sky near Gemini — one of the largest known optical nebular structures. The **[SII]/Hα ratio exceeds the canonical shock threshold of 0.4**, and BPT diagnostics favor shock excitation over photoionization; kinematics give a distance of **~160 pc** (28 pc physical extent), tentatively consistent with a pressure-driven supernova remnant of age **(6.8±1.8)×10⁴ yr**, though a diffuse-ionized-gas origin cannot yet be excluded.

### [Recovering Irreducible Three-Body Interactions with Lagrangian Neural Networks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13292v1)

Sena Kalabalik, Sukru Caglar, Abdullah Umut Hamzaogullari, Arkadas Ozakin
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: physics.atom-ph

The authors test whether a Lagrangian Neural Network — with kinetic energy fixed exactly and only the potential learned — can recover the **Axilrod-Teller three-body dispersion interaction**, a term that is *irreducibly* non-pairwise and so cannot be written as any sum of two-body potentials, purely from trajectory data. The learned potential correctly reproduces the interaction's **r⁻⁹ radial scaling**, angular dependence, and sign reversal, but is only identifiable once its dynamical signature exceeds the model's own fitting-error floor — achieved at 10×-amplified coupling but not at the real argon coupling strength, an honest **null result** at physical scale. Applying **symbolic regression** to the trained network with no assumed functional form recovers the exact analytic Axilrod-Teller expression intact.

![](figures/2610.13292/fig6_identifiability.pdf)

*Analytic three-body acceleration contribution plotted against the model's own fitting-error floor, for real vs. 10×-amplified coupling — showing the three-body signal is identifiable only once amplified, while at the true argon coupling it sits below the noise floor.*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Undiscovering Quark-gluon Plasma](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12882v1)

Thomas A. Trainor
**Primary category:** hep-ph

A single-author historical essay arguing that RHIC/LHC "QGP discovery" claims (flow, elliptic-flow parameter v2, jet quenching) emerged from a sociologically biased process within large collaborations, invoking Feynman's **"cargo cult science"**. It cites the 2008 disbanding of a dissenting working group that had found low-energy jets surviving in central collisions — contradicting the thermalized-bulk-matter narrative — and the later appearance of flow-like correlations in small systems at the LHC that undermined the original QGP-vs-cold-matter control logic. The piece presents no new measurement and is explicitly framed by its author (who was the disbanded group's convener) as a critique of how the scientific method functions inside large collaborations; it reads as a one-sided personal account rather than a balanced analysis, worth knowing about as commentary on big-collaboration science sociology rather than as physics evidence.
